## How the report is organized

The report brings together material concerns, matters needing another professional, near-term attention, routine maintenance, lower-priority observations, and inspection limitations. Relevant photographs support the written explanations and recommended next steps.

## Read beyond the summary

Start with the summary for orientation, then read the complete report for location, context, photographs, limitations, and recommendations. A priority label does not replace the explanation of what was observed and why it matters.

Photographs are not a complete inventory of every component. Weather, access, utilities, stored belongings, occupancy, safety, and property conditions can limit the inspection; an inaccessible area is not presumed satisfactory.

## What a sample cannot promise

Every property is different. A sample cannot promise an identical report length, photograph count, finding count, accessible area, or delivery time. It does not guarantee discovery of every concealed, intermittent, latent, or future condition.

The inspection does not provide engineering or structural certification, municipal code approval, repair bids, negotiation advice, a warranty, or automatic third-party reliance rights. The executed agreement, actual conditions, and delivered report control the inspection.
