## How It Differs From the Home Inspection

During a full residential inspection, Rivermark may operate accessible plumbing fixtures, observe visible drainage, and note visible piping or leakage.

Those observations do not establish the condition of the concealed underground building sewer.

A sewer scope is a separately contracted camera service. It may identify visible conditions in the observed portion of the line, but it does not replace a plumber or sewer specialist when further evaluation, access creation, cleaning, repair, or other work is needed.

## Scope and deliverable

The standard service covers one primary building sewer from one suitable access point toward the municipal or association connection, or toward the septic tank when applicable. A readily accessible cleanout is the normal access assumption. Controlled fixture flow is used only when safe and supported by training, equipment instructions, and property conditions.

You receive recorded video, concise written findings, selected images, the access point and direction of travel, approximate coverage, the endpoint or stopping point, and material limitations. A plumber or sewer specialist may be recommended when warranted. Additional lines, buildings, access points, or return visits require separate scope and pricing.

The deliverable is not an engineering analysis, repair estimate, excavation plan, or guarantee of the entire underground system.

## The standard service depends on a suitable, reasonably accessible cleanout.

The access point should not be buried, concealed, obstructed, painted shut, materially damaged, unsafe, or located where ordinary access would risk property damage.

The party arranging the service is responsible for lawful access and for providing known access information.

Rivermark may remove and replace a readily accessible cleanout cap or plug with ordinary hand tools when the inspector determines that doing so is safe and unlikely to damage the plumbing or property.

Rivermark does not:

- Excavate to find or create access
- Pull a toilet
- Force a damaged, corroded, painted, buried, or unsafe cap
- Dismantle plumbing to create access
- Promise roof-vent access
- Use an access point that presents an unreasonable damage or contamination risk



## Conditions the Service May Identify

The observed portion of the line may show conditions such as:

- Apparent displacement or offset
- Visible deterioration
- Root intrusion
- Obstruction or restricted advancement
- Standing water or an apparent low area
- Material transition
- Damage or deformation
- Conditions that warrant plumber or sewer-specialist evaluation
- Incomplete reach or uncertain configuration

The presence, absence, extent, cause, exact location, or future effect of a condition may not be determinable from the available access and camera view.

Rivermark does not imply that every underground condition will be visible or identified.

## Complete endpoint reach is not guaranteed.

Rivermark advances the camera only as far as the access, line conditions, and equipment allow safely.

Camera advancement stops when continuing may:

- Damage the plumbing or property
- Entrap or damage the camera
- Create an unreasonable contamination risk
- Exceed the equipment or service scope
- Require cleaning, dismantling, excavation, or repair

A blocked, unsafe, inaccessible, or incompletely observed line may still require a plumber or sewer specialist.

The report documents the available access, observed coverage, stopping point, and material limitations rather than representing a limited camera run as complete evaluation of the entire line.

## Important Boundaries

The service does not include:

- Drain cleaning
- Snaking or cabling
- Jetting
- Chemical treatment
- Sewer repair
- Excavation
- Toilet removal
- Septic tank, baffle, distribution-box, drainfield, or septic-system evaluation
- Guaranteed municipal or association endpoint
- Guaranteed underground location
- Capacity or flood testing
- Future-performance guarantee
- Engineering or code certification

The camera service may extend toward a septic tank when applicable, but it does not inspect the septic system itself.

## Sewer-Scope FAQs

### What part of the sewer line is inspected?

The standard service covers one primary building sewer from one suitable access point toward the expected municipal or association connection—or toward the septic tank when applicable.

The observed coverage and stopping point are documented.

### Is a sewer scope included in a home inspection?

No. A normal home inspection includes visible plumbing and functional drainage observations but does not establish the underground building sewer's condition.

The sewer scope is separately contracted.

### What access is required?

Rivermark's normal assumption is one suitable, readily accessible cleanout or other specifically agreed access point.

The access must be lawful, reasonably safe, and unlikely to be damaged by ordinary cap removal and camera use.

### What happens if there is no usable cleanout?

Rivermark may charge the published access-attempt fee and document the limitation.

Rivermark does not excavate, pull toilets, or dismantle plumbing to create access. A plumber may need to establish access before a later paid scope.

### Will the camera always reach the municipal connection?

No. Complete endpoint reach is not guaranteed.

The camera may be limited by access, configuration, offsets, obstruction, standing water, deterioration, line length, damage risk, contamination risk, or equipment limits.

### Does Rivermark pull toilets?

No. Toilet removal is outside the standard service.

### Does the service include drain cleaning?

No. Rivermark does not snake, cable, jet, chemically treat, clean, or repair the line.

### Does Rivermark inspect septic systems?

No. The camera may observe the accessible building sewer toward a septic tank, but Rivermark does not inspect the tank, baffles, distribution box, drainfield, soil absorption, capacity, or remaining life.

### Do I receive the video?

Yes. The customer receives the recorded video along with concise written findings, selected images, coverage, stopping-point information, and material limitations.

### Can I order a standalone sewer scope?

A standalone scope is a separate service. Confirm current availability and whether the property, access, territory, schedule, equipment, and service requirements fit.

### What if a plumber must establish access first?

A later scope after access is established is normally a new paid visit.

Any credit from the earlier access attempt is discretionary and is not promised.

### Does Rivermark locate the line underground?

Underground locating is not included. The report describes observed camera coverage and the stopping point without promising an exact underground location.
